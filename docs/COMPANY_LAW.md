# Company law

Binding on every deliverable. These are not style preferences — a change that breaks one
is wrong even if it looks good and passes every other gate.

## 1. The user never pays for their tools

Free means free. No meter, no tier, no trial, no "upgrade for" anything inside the apps.
Revenue lives in optional layers *around* the products — professional seats, concierge
services, zaps — never inside them. Copy that could be read as a paywall by a reasonable
skeptic is wrong copy.

## 2. Plain words, zero hype

Short declaratives, concrete nouns, no adjectives a skeptic could contest. A claim is
stated once, factually, and then lives in a FAQ answer — **the FAQ pattern beats the
boast pattern.**

Banned outright: exclamation points, emoji, "simply", "just", "easily", "seamless",
"revolutionary", "powerful", "supercharged", "ultimate", ALL-CAPS emphasis, slogan
strips, countdowns, "we're thrilled/excited to", stacked superlatives.

Test before shipping any sentence: *could a hostile bitcoiner quote this sarcastically?*
If yes, rewrite it. Second test: read it aloud flatly. If it only works with enthusiasm
behind it, it isn't house voice.

## 3. Records and models, not advice

Nothing published recommends financial, investment, or tax action. Every public page
carries this paragraph in the footer small print, **verbatim** — this exact text is the
canonical source; copy it from here:

> Twelve Words apps are for educational and informational purposes only. They do not constitute financial advice, investment advice, tax advice, accounting advice, or any other kind of professional advice. Figures presented are computed from entries you record and from prices that may not reflect actual market conditions. Past performance of any asset, including bitcoin, is not indicative of future results. Always consult a qualified financial advisor or tax professional before making investment or filing decisions. Use these tools at your own risk.

Adaptation of the first few words for a single-product page ("BitBooks is for…") is by
design. Shortening, paraphrasing, or omitting it is not.

## 4. Nostr is plumbing, never the pitch

Lead with outcomes: your key, your data, no kill switch. Protocol names — nostr, NIP-05,
relays, npub — belong in documentation and settings, never in a headline or lede.

## 5. Pseudonymity holds

No real names, locations, employers, schedules that fingerprint, or biographical detail
in any public artifact: copy, code, comments, commit messages, image metadata, or
screenshots. Strip EXIF. Check screenshots for usernames, file paths, and browser
profiles before they are committed.

## 6. Design language

Matte black ground, Inter, the Strike × Coinbase × Apple register — huge tight headlines,
generous space, few words. Parent-brand surfaces use neutral white CTAs; product accents
appear only in their own product's context. Production fidelity only: every UI deliverable
is screenshot-verified before it reaches the Founder. Skeleton quality never ships.

Full token set and component specs: `BRAND_SYSTEM.md`.

## 7. Founder-hours are the unit of account

The company runs on 10–15 hours a week and $0 capital. Cost every proposal in
founder-hours first and dollars second. A "free" idea that costs ten hours costs roughly
a fifth of the company's monthly capacity. Volume is never the goal; verified increments
are.
