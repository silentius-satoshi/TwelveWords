The idea

How the suite works.

01

One Recovery Key

Twelve words, generated once, written on paper. The same key signs you into every Twelve Words app. There is no password to reset and no account to recover — the key is the account.

02

Apps that never hold your data

Each app keeps its records on your device. If you turn on sync, entries travel between your devices in encrypted form — we can't read them, and we can't lose them.

03

If we vanish, your data doesn't

The code is public and the data stays with you. If Twelve Words disappeared tomorrow, your books, your plans, and your key would keep working without us.

Questions

Plain answers.

What is Twelve Words?
A small independent studio building personal-finance apps for bitcoiners — currently Personal ₿LOC, a credit-line planning tool, and BitBooks, a bookkeeping app in preview. The name is the mechanism: twelve words make one Recovery Key, and that key signs into every app.
What does it cost?
Nothing. The apps are free to use, with no meter and no paid tier inside them. If the company earns revenue, it comes from optional services around the apps — never from charging you for your own tools.
Do I need an account?
No. The apps work the moment they load. A Recovery Key is optional, and only matters when you want your records to follow you across devices — and it's a key you hold, not an account we host.
Where does my data live?
On your device. Nothing you record is sent to us — we don't run servers that could hold it. If you enable sync, your entries move between your devices in encrypted form over open, public infrastructure; the details live in each app's settings.
What's next?
BitBooks is being built in the open toward a public launch for the 2028 tax season. After that, more ledgers that sign in with the same twelve words. The work is posted as it ships.
